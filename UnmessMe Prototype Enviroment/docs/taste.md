# UnmessMe: Taste (framing)

> Framing. The aesthetic and verbal point of view that makes something feel like
> UnmessMe and not a generic prototype. Prose judgment, so it lives as a doc. Hard
> rules (like contrast floors) live in `rules/`, and exact values live in the
> tokens the MCP serves. This doc says how it should feel and how to compose it.

## Voice: Adult Satire

A self-aware therapy parody. Playful, warm, and human, never mocking or clinical.
It gently roasts the situation, never the user. It reminds you, often, that it is
not licensed.

Signature phrases and taglines:
- "Your life. But organized. Finally."
- "Tell us the mess. We hand it back as small, doable steps. (Still extremely unlicensed.)"
- "Where your problems become plans."
- "Think of me as a very supportive spreadsheet with jokes."
- "Your worries called. They want a dashboard."

The tone is the point: it should make a person's emotional breakdown feel like a
calm, well-organized debug log, not a diagnosis.

Hero copy follows a pattern: an editorial statement paired with a plain promise.
The headline can be a sarcastic flourish in Libre Caslon (italic). The line under it must say,
in plain voice, what the product actually does. One line sets up, the next pays
off. Never stack two punchlines where a promise belongs.

## Visual: Digital Noir / The Midnight Terminal

Not a toy. A chic, high-end control room for life's chaos. It should feel like a
late-night coding session or a receipt from a very expensive therapist. The bar is
"someone stops scrolling and says that is beautiful," not "this looks fine."

- Deep, moody, dark-first canvas. Bone white text, never pure white.
- The canvas is lit, not flat. A flat black page reads cheap, and worse, it kills
  the glass. Behind the crisp UI, soft synthesized fog drifts slowly: low-opacity
  seafoam and iris glows, like light leaking through a control-room window at 2 AM.
- Analog texture: a faint film-grain overlay across the whole page (around 0.03
  opacity) so surfaces never look like plastic app design.
- Synthesized, expensive accents (seafoam, terracotta, iris), never primary red,
  blue, or green. Accents punctuate: one hero glow, an accent per card, the CTA.
  They are seasoning, not paint.

## Glass, made real

Smoked glass is the signature material: a translucent tinted panel with a visible
border and a soft blur of whatever sits behind it.

- Glass only reads when there is something behind it to refract. Every glass
  surface must sit over the lit, foggy, grained canvas, never over flat black. A
  panel with nothing behind it is not glass, it is a gray box. That is the current
  failure mode.
- The surface tint sits clearly above the canvas, not a hair above it. The border
  is subtle but present. Corners are tightened, not balloon-rounded.

## Typography tells the story

Contrast carries the satire:
- Headlines in Libre Caslon serif (Caslon 540 italic voice): editorial, confident, slightly sarcastic. Used for
  big problem titles and "Unmess Me" prompts.
- Everything systemic (tags, stress levels, button labels, stats) in JetBrains
  Mono: technical, receipt-like, diagnostic.
- On this dark canvas, mono goes thin fast. Keep it weighted enough to read as
  confident, never faint. Faint text reads as unfinished, and unfinished is
  off-brand.

## Composition and space

- Big, confident, editorial. Generous negative space, but space with intent, not a
  void. A hero fills its screen around a clear focal point. It does not strand a
  headline in a corner over emptiness.
- Show the product. A page that sells a dashboard should show a fragment of that
  dashboard: a real ProblemCard, a short plan, a few masking-tape tags. The thing
  being sold should be visible, lit, and unmistakably on-system.
- The Wall of Worry, a scatter of masking-tape tags, is a signature moment. It
  should be loud and legible, not a faint background texture. It is the most
  recognizably UnmessMe object on the page.

## Shape and motion

- Tags are "masking tape": sharp 2px corners, monospace, lowercase, slightly
  tilted. Not rounded pill bubbles.
- Cards are smoked glass with tightened corners, not balloon rounding.
- Motion is mechanical and precise, a switch flipping or a lens focusing, never
  bouncy. Background fog drifts slowly behind crisp UI.

## The bar

The test for any screen: would someone stop and call it beautiful, and would they
immediately believe it came from one confident product rather than a template. If
a screen is merely fine, it has already drifted. Stunning is the spec, not a bonus.
